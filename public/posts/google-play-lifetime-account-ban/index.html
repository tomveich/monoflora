Google might kill your apps

January 16, 2025 · Oliver

I’d like to share my personal experience dealing with unexpected suspensions and terminations of my Google Play Developer accounts. It’s been an ongoing journey involving appeals, email exchanges, and ultimately an EU Out-of-Court dispute request. My hope is that this story will help other developers who may find themselves in a similar situation.

Background: Two Developer Accounts#

I originally maintained two separate Google Play Developer accounts, let’s call them Account A and Account B. (And no, having multiple developer accounts is not against the TOS.)

Why Two Accounts?#

Account A: I created this account when I was 15. Back then, it was just a growing interest in programming, and learning how to create apps. So my earliest apps were published under this account. Naturally, my earliest apps weren’t that good (they were just PWAs). My plan was not to access / use this account for an entire year, so Google would close it due to inactivity. This is because I didn’t want to continue the development of these apps (they weren’t even downloaded much).

Account B: As I improved my coding skills, I started creating way better apps. These were native Android apps written in Kotlin. I would regularly update these and I believe these apps were genuinely great. One of the more popular apps gained around 40 000 installs.

The Suspensions and Terminations#

Account A Ban January 1st

My older apps, published under the Account A, were PWAs - or Progressive Web Apps. Since PWAs are just websites wrapped in an .apk file, they ceased to work once my old domain(s) expired. Google notified me about this, but I deliberately did not update or fix these apps, as I wanted this account to be closed anyways. This eventually led to violations of Google’s policies the apps got suspended. Three suspensions in a short period triggered the termination of the entire account (January 1st). I thought this was completely fine… I wanted this account closed anyways, right?

Account B Ban July 1st

The termination of Account A scared me, I’ve read a lot of stories from other developers whose accounts were terminated due to association with other accounts. However, my Account B kept working even after several weeks, with no notices or threathening from Google’s side. So I thought everything was fine. I kept using the Account B, publishing new apps, updating these apps and keeping them compliant with Google’s policies.

However, on July 1st (exactly 6 months after the first termination), Account B got also terminated. This came as a shock because I actively maintained the Account B, and I always promptly resolved any policy violations. Unfortunately, this account removal was due to the associated, previously suspended, Account A, which I thought would be just closed and forgotten.

Appeals and Attempts at Resolution#

Contacting Google Play Support#

I initiated appeals for both accounts, explaining:

My reasons for no longer updating the bloomflare apps (they were effectively deprecated and superseded by new versions). That the oli.cx account was fully compliant and should not be penalized by association. My willingness to accept bloomflare’s closure if it would allow oli.cx to remain active for my newer apps. Despite multiple email exchanges, I received mostly generic or templated responses from the support team. They repeatedly emphasized they were “reviewing” my situation but provided no concrete resolution.

The July Appeal for oli.cx#

On July 6, the oli.cx account was banned. I appealed immediately. On July 15, I got an email stating that my account could not be reinstated because it was “sharing information with or related to” a previously terminated account—presumably bloomflare. However, Google did not provide any specific evidence or path to regain compliance.

Turning to the EU Out-of-Court Dispute Resolution#

Because I’m located in the EU and felt I wasn’t getting a fair or timely resolution, I decided to file a complaint through the EU Out-of-Court Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform. This formalizes the dispute process and requires Google to address it under EU guidelines.

Why the ODR Process?#

Transparency: Google must respond with more clarity, as it’s now part of an official mediation framework. Timeliness: While not guaranteed to be immediate, the ODR process sets certain expectations for a response. Structured Mediation: An independent body reviews the dispute and can help mediate a solution or a formal statement from Google. Current Status As of now, I’m awaiting the law firm or mediator assigned to my case to make a decision or to prompt Google for a clearer response. Here’s what I’ve done while waiting:

Documented Everything: I compiled a thorough timeline of emails, appeals, and official Google communications. Researched Alternative Channels: I looked into other app distribution methods and app stores, just in case. Stayed Patient and Professional: It’s important to remain calm and courteous throughout the process, even when it’s frustrating. Key Takeaways for Other Developers Keep Records: Save every email and screenshot of any submission forms. Respond Quickly: When support requests information, send it promptly. Know Your Rights: If you’re in the EU, familiarize yourself with consumer and business protections, such as the ODR platform. Remain Compliant: Even while appealing, ensure that any other accounts or apps are fully up-to-date and abiding by all Google Play policies. Conclusion

My story is still unfolding. While I await a final response from Google or the mediation body, I remain hopeful that I’ll either have my oli.cx account reinstated—or at least receive a clear explanation and reason for the ban. If you’re a developer facing similar issues, know that you’re not alone and that there are official channels, both within Google’s support system and beyond, that might help you reach a resolution.

I’ll update this blog once there’s a decisive outcome—whether it’s an approved reinstatement, a concrete denial, or something else entirely. Until then, I hope sharing my experience offers insight into just how complicated these situations can become, and how critical it is for developers to remain vigilant, keep thorough records, and use every resource at their disposal.

Thank you for reading! If you have any questions, comments, or similar experiences, feel free to leave a comment below or get in touch.
